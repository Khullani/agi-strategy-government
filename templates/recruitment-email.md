# Recruitment Email Template

Use this as a starting point for inviting participants to your first cohort. Three things matter: explicit framing as an experiment, named cohort composition (without naming individuals), and a specific, immovable time commitment.

Keep it short. Senior people decide in three sentences whether they're in.

---

## Template

> Subject: An experiment — five Wednesdays on [your salience]
>
> [First name],
>
> I'm running a small five-week salon on [your salience — e.g., "the governance of frontier AI in state government," "AI in municipal procurement," "what the next eighteen months of state AI legislation actually means"]. The format is deliberate: four to six senior practitioners from different sides of the problem, ninety minutes on Zoom, Chatham House Rules, gallery view, real cold-calling, real disagreement. Curriculum is adapted from BlueDot Impact's AGI Strategy series, localized for [your jurisdiction / sector].
>
> The cohort I'm building includes [one-line description of each participant's role/lever, anonymized — e.g., "a state legislative chief of staff, a senior digital services technologist, a private investor with exposure to AI infrastructure, and a national-security scholar"]. I want you in this room because [one sentence on the specific perspective you bring]. The friction between the four of you is the point.
>
> The sessions are [day] [time] beginning [date] and running through [date]. Five sessions. Reading load is about ninety minutes a week, curated tightly. Not a webinar. Not a training. A room.
>
> This is a first cohort, which means it's an experiment. I'm asking you to do me the courtesy of telling me when something isn't working. In return, I'll do the work of making it worth your five Wednesdays.
>
> Will you be in?
>
> — K.

---

## Notes on use

- **Send individually.** Not a group email. Each person gets a version tuned to why you specifically want them.
- **Name the experiment.** "First cohort" + "experiment" buys you the latitude to change things on the fly. It also signals seriousness — you're not selling a finished product.
- **Be specific about the composition.** Senior people decide whether to participate based on who else is in the room. Anonymize names if you must, but describe the seats clearly.
- **Make the time commitment immovable.** "Five Wednesdays from X to Y, ninety minutes." Don't offer to reschedule. People who can't make it weren't going to make it anyway, and the ones who can will rearrange their calendar because the room is worth it.
- **Don't oversell.** No "transformative" or "exclusive" or any of that vocabulary. State the format and the friction. Senior people respond to seriousness, not to marketing.

## What not to include

- No bio. They know who you are or they can find out.
- No fee. The first cohort is free. (You may charge later cohorts; that's a different conversation.)
- No call to a website. Reply by email or not at all.
- No sign-off. Direct close.
