# Weekly Session Email Template

Sent the Thursday before each Wednesday session — seven days of lead time on readings is the right amount for senior professionals.

Plain prose. No sign-off block. No header image. The email itself is part of the curriculum — its tone sets the tone for the session.

---

## Structure

1. **Two-to-four sentence framing paragraph.** What this session is about, what tension it surfaces, what came up in the previous session that this builds on.
2. **The reading list.** Each entry follows the format in [`reading-list-format.md`](./reading-list-format.md).
3. **One closing sentence.** What you expect them to bring on Wednesday. Always action-oriented, never reflective. "Bring a decision, not a summary."

---

## Example

> Subject: Wednesday [date] — [Session title]
>
> This week we work the toolkit. Three readings, two of them in deliberate tension — Reading 2 makes the optimistic case for a stack of voluntary and existing-authority instruments; Reading 3 walks through what happened to one of those instruments when it actually got tried in California. Read them as the bright and dark sides of the same theory.
>
> Reading 1 (Toolkit anchor) — ~45 minutes
> Governing AI with Existing Authorities — CSET (Corrigan, Daniels, Kahn, Hague), July 2024
> https://cset.georgetown.edu/publication/governing-ai-with-existing-authorities/
> Maps the existing legal and regulatory authorities the U.S. federal government already has to govern AI — from procurement to FTC enforcement to sector-specific agencies. The frame: most of what people call "AI governance" can be done without new legislation.
> Discussion prompt: Pick one authority from this report that's already operating in your seat. What would it take to use it deliberately on an AI deployment in the next ninety days?
>
> Reading 2 (Philosophical anchor) — ~30 minutes
> d/acc: one year later — Vitalik Buterin, January 2025
> https://vitalik.eth.limo/general/2025/01/05/dacc2.html
> Defensive accelerationism as a frame: differentially advancing defensive technology relative to offensive. Asks how civic and information infrastructure can be hardened against AI-enabled threats without slowing the underlying capability curve.
> Discussion prompt: Where does d/acc cash out in your jurisdiction? Name one defensive capability that's underfunded relative to its strategic importance.
>
> Reading 3 (Cautionary case) — ~25 minutes
> Clawed — Dean Ball, August 2024
> https://www.hyperdimensional.co/p/clawed
> A close reading of how SB1047 — the most-watched state AI bill of the year — collapsed despite serious bipartisan support. Read it as the dark side of Reading 2: the same toolkit, the opposite outcome.
> Discussion prompt: Same toolkit, opposite outcome. What constraint, if any, makes the difference?
>
> Wednesday's session will pivot accordingly. We'll work the toolkit by name, locate where each of you sits, and end with a specific Monday-morning move tied to a specific lever. Bring decisions, not summaries.
>
> See you Wednesday.

---

## Notes on use

- **Send Thursday.** Six days in front of the session. Senior people block weekend reading time on Friday.
- **No greeting line.** "Gentlemen," "Team," "Everyone," all soften the room. Just start.
- **No sign-off.** Direct close. "See you Wednesday." The tone is collegial-but-serious.
- **No reading apologies.** Never write "I know the readings are long this week" or "feel free to skim." You set the floor at the level you want the conversation. They will rise to it.
- **One discussion prompt per reading.** Sharp, specific, seat-anchored where possible. The prompts are the conversation's spine.
- **State the next session's frame.** The closing sentence should be a glimpse into what Wednesday will do with these readings. It pre-loads the discussion.
