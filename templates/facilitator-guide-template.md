# Facilitator Guide Template

The facilitator guide is internal to you. The participants never see it. Its purpose is to make you a calmer, sharper facilitator in the moment — when the discussion stalls, when someone hasn't done the readings, when a participant pushes back harder than you expected, when you have nine minutes left and three things still to cover.

Length: six to twelve pages, depending on session complexity. Structured by time block.

---

## Standard structure

### Page 1 — Session at a Glance

A one-page summary so you can refresh thirty minutes before going live.

- **Session title and number**
- **Date and time**
- **Participants** (names, seats, what each one brings)
- **The frame** — one sentence on what this session is about
- **The tension** — one sentence on the disagreement you expect to surface
- **What success looks like** — three bullets, the outcomes you want by the end of the session

### Page 2 — Run-of-Show

A minute-by-minute schedule. Be specific.

```
0:00–0:05  Welcome, Chatham House reminder, cold-call warning
0:05–0:08  Restate the course arc, locate today
0:08–0:25  My briefing — 17 minutes, slides 5–14
0:25–0:30  First cold call → [Participant]
0:30–0:55  Reading 1 discussion
0:55–1:10  Reading 2 discussion + cross-reference to Reading 1
1:10–1:25  Activity / tabletop / decision exercise
1:25–1:30  Round-robin close — one sentence each on Monday move
```

### Pages 3–N — Beat-by-beat notes

For each major block (briefing, each reading, the activity, the close), write a page that includes:

- **What you're trying to do in this block** (one sentence)
- **The exact cold-call you'll open with** (in quotes, so you can read it if your brain freezes)
- **What to listen for** — three to five things you want the room to surface
- **Cross-references** — when this block should be tied back to something earlier
- **Escape valves** — what to do if discussion stalls, what to do if someone hasn't done the readings, what to do if discussion goes off the rails

### Final page — Emergency notes

A grab bag of things to deploy when needed:

- **If discussion stalls:** "Let me put a sharper version of the question on the table — [sharper version]"
- **If two participants are dominating:** "I want to hear from [quieter participant] before we move on"
- **If discussion gets personal:** "Let me redirect to the substance. What's the *argument* you'd make against that?"
- **If someone hasn't read:** "Walk me through how that lands from your seat — you don't need to have read the piece to tell me what's true from where you sit"
- **If you're running long:** "We have eight minutes. Last cross-cut — [pre-prepared cross-cut question]"

## What makes a good cold call

The cold call is the highest-leverage facilitator move you have. It signals:

1. You know who is in the room and what they bring.
2. You expect them to bring it.
3. You are not going to let anyone hide.

A good cold call has three parts:

> **[Participant's seat], I want to come to you first.** *(Signals: I chose you, deliberately.)*
>
> **You're the only person in this room who has actually [specific thing they have done].** *(Signals: I did my homework. I am not flattering you.)*
>
> **[Specific question anchored to that experience.]** *(Signals: I want a specific answer, not a reflection.)*

### Examples

> Colorado, I want to come to you first. You're the only person in this room who has actually been the procurement counterparty in a contract that the EU AI Act would have classified as high-risk. What did the contract require that the Act doesn't?

> Illinois, before we get to Reading 2, I want your read on Reading 1's central claim. You watched a bill on this exact topic go through committee last quarter. Does the author's frame survive contact with what you actually saw happen?

> PE, you've underwritten an AI-adjacent investment in the past six months. The author of this piece is arguing the capital cycle is the binding constraint. Is he right, or is he flattering himself?

### Examples to avoid

> So, what did everyone make of the readings?  *(Open-ended, ungrounded, signals you didn't prepare.)*

> Colorado, what do you think?  *(Anchored to a seat but not to a specific argument or experience.)*

> Illinois, you must have a lot to say about Reading 2!  *(Flattering, presumptuous, soft.)*

## Notes on use

- **Write the guide as you build the deck.** They co-evolve. If you can't write the cold-call language for a slide, the slide isn't ready.
- **Print it.** Have it on paper next to your laptop. You will not flip tabs during a cold call.
- **Note time-checks.** Put a literal "(check time here)" in the guide at the 25-minute, 60-minute, and 80-minute marks. You will forget otherwise.
- **Update it after the session.** Note what worked and what didn't. The guide for next cohort's Session 4 is this cohort's facilitator guide + your scribbles.
